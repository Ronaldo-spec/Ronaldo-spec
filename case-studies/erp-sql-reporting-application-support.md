# ERP SQL Reporting & Application Support — Sanitized Case Study

> This case study is sanitized. Company names, client names, database structures, operational figures, and sensitive business data are intentionally removed or generalized.

## Summary

This case study demonstrates my work in supporting ERP users, analyzing operational issues, validating business data, and preparing SQL-based reports for decision support.

The work combined business analysis, application support, SQL reporting, troubleshooting, user communication, and process documentation.

## Business Context

A business team needed clearer operational visibility from its ERP system. Users often required reports that could summarize transactions, operational status, document progress, and data conditions across different business functions.

At the same time, users also raised system issues that required investigation across workflow, data entries, document status, and report logic.

## Problem

The main challenges were:

- Users needed reports that matched actual business processes.
- Existing data needed to be validated before being used for decision-making.
- Some system issues required checking transaction flow and related database records.
- Non-technical users needed clear explanations about what happened, what was fixed, and what the impact was.
- Business processes needed to be documented so future changes and support work could be handled more consistently.

## My Role

I worked as an ERP Business Analyst / Application Support Specialist, responsible for:

- Understanding user needs and operational context.
- Translating business questions into report requirements.
- Creating and validating SQL-based reports.
- Investigating system or transaction issues.
- Checking data consistency and document status.
- Communicating root cause, solution, impact, and current status to affected users.
- Preparing workflow documentation, SOP references, and process notes.

## Approach

### 1. Requirement clarification

I started by clarifying the user’s actual business question instead of directly writing a query. This helped avoid reports that were technically correct but operationally misleading.

### 2. Data and workflow checking

I checked the relevant ERP document flow, transaction status, date logic, linked records, and report assumptions before preparing the final output.

### 3. SQL report preparation

I created SQL queries to retrieve, aggregate, filter, and validate operational data. The logic was reviewed against expected business rules to reduce incorrect reporting conclusions.

### 4. Issue analysis and communication

For reported issues, I inspected related data or workflow conditions, identified possible causes, coordinated the fix when needed, and explained the result in practical language for users.

### 5. Documentation

I documented key business rules, workflow logic, assumptions, and support notes so future users or team members could understand how the process worked.

## Deliverables

- SQL-based operational reports.
- Data validation notes.
- Root cause analysis summaries.
- User-facing issue explanations.
- Workflow documentation.
- SOP/process notes.
- Report logic clarification.

## Tools & Skills

## Result

The work helped users access clearer operational reports, reduced ambiguity in system issue handling, and improved communication between business users and technical stakeholders.

The biggest value was not only creating reports, but ensuring that the data, logic, and explanation matched the actual business process.
